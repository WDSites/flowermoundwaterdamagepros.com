Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Flower Mound, TX

Commercial water damage restoration in Flower Mound, TX means extracting standing water from a business property, drying the walls, floors and ceilings, and documenting the readings for the owner. Our dispatch line runs 24/7. If water is moving right now, close the nearest shut-off valve and call, then tell the dispatcher what the space is and where it sits.

What We Do for a Business Property

We do four things on a commercial job. We pull standing water with pumps and extractors, dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, take moisture readings with meters, and photograph the affected areas. Those photos and readings go to the owner or property manager for their records.

We do not repair the source. A plumber fixes a failed supply line, a roofer fixes a leaking membrane, and a fire sprinkler contractor handles a sprinkler head. Our crew starts once the water is stopped, or helps you find the right shut-off while you wait for that trade.

The rebuild that follows drying, such as new drywall, ceiling tile or flooring, is covered under water damage repair. The drying phase itself is explained in detail on the structural drying page.

Safety in the First Hour

  1. If water is near a panel, outlet, or you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 or the utility first.
  2. Move people out of wet areas and away from sagging ceiling tile, which can drop without warning.
  3. Shut off the water supply to the affected area, or the building main if the source is unclear.
  4. Switch off breakers only from a dry panel while standing on a dry surface.
  5. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, then call dispatch.

If the water came from a drain or toilet line, treat it as contaminated and keep people out of it. The sewage backup cleanup page covers that case. Water that entered from outdoors, such as a creek overflow or street runoff, is different again, and flood damage restoration explains it.

How the Work Goes

1

Describe and locate

Dispatch asks what the space is, where the water is and where the entrance is. Give a cross street or highway reference so the crew finds the right door.

2

Extract

Pumps and extractors remove standing water. Wet carpet, pad and ceiling tile are lifted where they cannot be saved.

3

Measure

Moisture meters and a thermal camera map where water went, including inside walls and under flooring.

4

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run on the affected rooms. Readings are retaken on each visit until they match an unaffected reference.

5

Document

The owner or property manager receives photos and moisture readings for their records.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference to ask about for a commercial job, and any restorer should explain how its crews use it. It defines clean, gray and contaminated water, which is why the first question on any job is where the water came from.

Keeping a Business Running During the Work

If the building has several rooms, the work can often be phased so one room is dried while another stays in use. That depends on where the water went, whether air movers can run without blocking exits, and how loud the equipment is. The crew can talk through the options once someone has seen the space.

Tell us who has authority to approve work. Often that is the owner, and sometimes it is a property manager or the person holding the lease. We will explain scope and price before work starts, and we work with whichever contact you name.

What Changes the Cost

No honest price can be given before someone sees the space. These factors move it most:

  • Square footage that is wet, and how many rooms or units are involved.
  • Water category: clean supply water, gray water or a contaminated source.
  • Materials involved, such as carpet tile, drywall, ceiling grid or concrete floor.
  • How long the water sat before the call.
  • Equipment count and how many days drying takes.
  • Access, such as after-hours entry or phasing around a working business.

Dispatch explains the scope and price before work starts, and you can compare it with what your insurer says it will cover.

Insurance for a Business Property

The Texas Department of Insurance guidance we reference covers homeowner and renter policies. A business owner should check their commercial policy and ask their agent how it treats sudden water, gradual leaks, outside flooding and sewer or drain backup. The wording can differ from a home policy.

Whatever the policy says, photograph the damage before anything moves, keep damaged materials until the insurer says otherwise, and keep receipts for temporary repairs. We provide photos and moisture readings for claims, but we do not decide coverage. That is between you, your agent and the insurer.

Mistakes to Avoid

Do not wait until morning while water runs into a ceiling, and do not put paint or new tile over a wall nobody has read with a meter. Moisture left in a wall or under flooring can keep a smell and a stain alive long after the visible water is gone. The Texas Department of Insurance notes that mold can start growing within a day after a leak, so a quick call and a measured dry-out matter.
