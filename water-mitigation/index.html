Mitigation is not repair and it is not cleanup. It is the work that decides whether repair is small or large. Every hour that drywall, insulation and flooring stay wet, the damage moves further into the structure.

Wet Spots, Soft Walls and Damp Smells: Is That Mitigation?

Most people do not search for mitigation. They search for what they see: a wet spot in a closet ceiling, carpet that squishes underfoot, a wall that feels cool and damp, a smell near the water heater. A damp ceiling stain, a floor that feels soft or an odor that returns after you mop all point to water that is still inside the structure.

The trade name for the fix is water mitigation, also called structural drying. It means finding every wet surface, including the ones you cannot see, and removing the water before it causes lasting damage or mold. If any of those signs match your home, call now and describe what you see.

When Water Is an Emergency: The First Hour

Do these in order while you wait

  1. Stop the source. Shut off the main water valve or the valve at the fixture.
  2. Check electrical safety. Do not step into standing water near outlets, appliances or a panel. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground.
  3. If you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 or the utility first, then call the restoration line.
  4. Lift valuables, shoes and electronics off wet floors. Pull rugs and loose items off damp carpet.
  5. Keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated.
  6. Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved.

The Texas Department of Insurance gives similar advice: shut off the water, remove standing water, dry the area quickly and move wet items to a dry place. TDI also warns that mold can start growing within a day after a leak, which is why the first 24 hours matter.

Drying Comes Next

After the standing water and wet materials are out, the structure still holds moisture in wall cavities, subfloors and insulation. Crews map it with meters, set air movers and dehumidifiers and record readings at each visit until the material is back in the normal range. The full drying process has its own page on structural drying and dehumidification.

Structural Drying: What Gets Dried and How

Structural drying covers everything the water touched, not only what you can see. Wall cavities, the space under cabinets, the subfloor below the carpet and the insulation in the attic can all hold moisture. Crews drill small weep holes or lift toe-kicks to push air into cavities, or remove wet drywall when drying in place will not work.

The crew records moisture readings at set points on day one and again each visit. Those readings are the proof that drying is finished, and they are the same readings your insurance adjuster may ask to see. Equipment stays until every reading is back in the normal range for that material.

What Affects the Cost of Water Mitigation

  • Water category: clean, gray or sewage water.
  • Square footage affected and how far it traveled.
  • Materials involved: drywall, hardwood, tile, insulation.
  • Drying days and the amount of equipment needed.
  • How quickly mitigation began after the loss.
