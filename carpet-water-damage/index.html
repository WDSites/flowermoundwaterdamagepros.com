Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Flower Mound, TX

Wet carpet can often be saved if the water was clean and caught early. Call dispatch and get the carpet and pad checked with a meter before you decide.

Carpet water damage restoration in Flower Mound, TX starts with finding out how wet the carpet and the pad underneath really are. Dispatch is 24/7. If carpet is soaked, stop the water, take photos, and call so a crew can extract the water and measure before you pull anything up.

Carpet, Pad and Subfloor Are Three Layers

Carpet is only the top layer. Under it sits a cushion, usually foam, then the subfloor, and the carpet edge is held by tack strip along the walls. Water that soaks the surface drops through the carpet weave into the pad, which acts like a sponge.

Water also wicks. Carpet and drywall pull moisture sideways and upward, so a wet patch near a wall can darken the bottom inch or two of drywall and the baseboard. A good inspection checks the wall base, not just the floor.

Dry in Place or Replace

Clean water caught quickly can often be handled by extracting, lifting the carpet, and drying the pad and subfloor with air movers and dehumidifiers. Sometimes the carpet is lifted from tack strip, floated on a raised support, dried and then re-stretched.

Carpet drying decisions are described in ANSI/IICRC S500, so ask a restorer whether it works from that reference. It sorts water into clean, gray and contaminated, and that sorting drives most carpet decisions.

The pad usually goes. Foam pad is inexpensive compared with carpet, it holds water against the subfloor, and it dries slowly. Many jobs save the carpet and replace the pad, but the call depends on the water category and readings.

Safety Before You Touch Wet Carpet

  1. If water is near outlets, a panel or an appliance, or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility first.
  2. Stop the source: shut the valve or fixture supply feeding the leak.
  3. Keep power off to wet outlets. Turn breakers off only from a dry panel and a dry floor.
  4. Do not use a household vacuum on soaked carpet.
  5. Lift furniture legs off the carpet, and set wood or foil under legs that must stay.

If the water came from a toilet, a floor drain or a backed up line, do not walk through it. See sewage backup cleanup for that case. Water that came from outside the home is covered under flood damage restoration.

How We Dry and Restore Carpet

1

Extract

An extractor pulls water out of the carpet and pad. This removes far more than towels or a shop vacuum.

2

Check

A moisture meter checks the carpet, pad, subfloor and baseboard. A thermal camera shows where the damp patch ends.

3

Lift

The carpet is lifted from tack strip along the wet walls. Wet pad is cut out and removed.

4

Dry

Air movers blow across the subfloor and under the lifted carpet. Dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air.

5

Reset

Once readings are normal, new pad goes down and the carpet is power-stretched back onto tack strip.

Drying usually takes several days, and the crew rechecks readings on each visit. Our structural drying page explains the equipment in more detail.

Stairs, Bedrooms and Living Areas

Bedrooms and living rooms are usually open floors with a single carpet piece, which makes lifting and re-stretching straightforward. Stairs are harder because carpet wraps each tread and riser, and water can follow the stair stringers into the wall below.

If water came through a ceiling from an upper room, wet carpet in the room below may be the second sign of a bigger issue. The path of the water matters more than the room, so describe where it started. A roof leak or a bath above may be the source.

Why Time Matters

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak, and it advises pulling up wet carpets and rugs and moving wet items to a dry area. That is why a call within hours matters more than finishing a cleanup yourself.

Texas licensing guidance from TDLR also says to keep indoor humidity at or below 50 percent, using air conditioning or a dehumidifier. After carpet dries, a low-humidity room helps keep it from smelling damp. If a musty smell continues, see mold remediation.

What Changes the Cost

Price depends on the scope, so a person has to see the carpet. These factors move it most:

  • Water category, which decides whether the carpet can stay.
  • Square footage of wet carpet and pad.
  • How many days the carpet sat wet.
  • Whether baseboard, drywall or subfloor also got wet.
  • Carpet type and whether it can be lifted and re-stretched.
  • Equipment count and days of drying.

Dispatch explains the scope and price before work starts. For carpet that must be replaced, ask whether new carpet is part of the quote or handled separately.

Insurance and Carpet

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a broken washer hose. Gradual leaks and seepage are generally excluded. A carpet that stayed damp for weeks may be treated differently from one soaked yesterday, so check your policy.

TDI also advises photographing the damage before repairs and keeping damaged items until the adjuster sees them. Photograph the wet carpet, cut a small sample if asked, and keep receipts for temporary repairs. We provide photos and moisture readings for claims.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the pad stay wet because the top of the carpet feels dry.
  • Putting a fan on the carpet without checking the pad and subfloor.
  • Running a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Covering a damp carpet with a rug.
