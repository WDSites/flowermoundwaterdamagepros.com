Storm water damage cleanup in Flower Mound, TX is the work after a storm has pushed rain, hail or runoff into your home. A storm leaves several problems at once. Some are urgent, some can wait until morning, and some belong to your insurer first. Sorting them in the right order is most of the job.

Resist the urge to start with the visible mess. A limb on the lawn is dramatic but does not damage anything further tonight. An open window with rain blowing through it does. Cover openings first, then deal with water, then everything else.

Storm Safety: Before You Touch Anything

First hour after the storm

If power is out, treat every wet outlet and cord as live once it returns.

  • Do not wade through water that reaches outlets, appliances or the panel. Leave and call 911 or the utility.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Stay away from downed lines and anything touching them, including wet fences and puddles near them.
  • Wear boots and gloves around broken glass and wet debris.
  • Move people and pets out of rooms with hanging ceilings or bulging drywall.

The Texas Department of Insurance says to prevent further damage by removing standing water and covering broken windows and holes against rain. It also says to keep every receipt, because the policy may pay for temporary repairs. Buy plastic sheeting, boards and tape, and save the receipt.

Wind-Driven Rain, Hail and Broken Openings

Wind pushes rain sideways, so water reaches places a straight-down storm never touches. Window sills, weep holes, garage door seals and door thresholds can all admit water, and it often runs behind baseboards or under flooring before you notice. A damp baseboard after a storm is worth a moisture meter reading even if the carpet feels dry.

Hail and wind can open the roof or punch through a skylight, and the leak then follows the path described on our roof leak water damage page. For this page the question is simpler: what got wet, and has it been metered. Cover the opening, photograph it from outside if it is safe, and keep any broken pieces as evidence.

Water That Came In With the Storm

The National Weather Service Fort Worth 1991 to 2020 normal for annual precipitation at DFW is 37.01 inches. A single storm can deliver a meaningful share of that in hours, which is more than gutters, grading and door seals are built to handle on a bad day. That is a reason to look at where water entered, not only where it ended up.

Flower Mound's southern boundary follows Grapevine Lake and Marshall Creek. If your property sits near either, check the FEMA flood map for your address. When water rises from outside and enters at ground level, that is a flood event, and flood damage restoration explains how that differs, including why home policies treat flood separately.

Rain that entered through a garage or an attached porch can also carry mud and debris. Treat it as contaminated until a technician says otherwise, and keep children and pets away from it.

Working Your Storm Claim

The Texas Department of Insurance says to file a claim as soon as you can if hail or strong winds damaged your home. Photograph and record video of damage inside and outside, list it, and keep damaged items until the insurer says you can discard them. Our crew adds moisture readings and dated photos you can hand to the adjuster.

  • Be present when the adjuster visits so every area is seen, and keep a log of who you spoke with.
  • If the storm makes the home unsafe, most policies cover some living expenses, so keep those receipts.
  • Get more than one repair bid, and check references and phone numbers.
  • Do not pay in full up front, and do not sign a contract with blank spaces.
  • State law makes waiving a homeowners deductible illegal, so be wary of any contractor who offers to.

Wind and hail coverage on a home policy should cover hail damage, according to TDI. Flood is different and needs its own policy, so check yours before assuming outside water is covered.

Mistakes That Cost People After a Storm

  • Handing a contractor a signed blank contract in the rush.
  • Throwing away damaged items before the adjuster has seen them.
  • Cleaning up first and photographing later.
  • Running a shop vacuum in water near outlets.
  • Waiting for the weekend because the water looks like it stopped.
