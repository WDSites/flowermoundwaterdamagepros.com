Safety Steps Before the Crew Arrives

First Safety Steps in Lake Forest

These limit the damage while you wait. None of them should put you in danger.

Stop the waterClose the main shutoff, or the valve at the failed fixture, if you can reach it safely and the floor is dry.
Mind the electricityStay out of standing water near outlets, cords and appliances. Do not flip a breaker while standing in water.
Photograph firstTake photos and a short video of the damage before anything is moved. They support your insurance record.
Keep people clearKeep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, toilet or outside. Call 911 or the utility first for gas, sparks or smoke.

If the water came from a drain or from outside, keep everyone out of it and tell the dispatcher. Contaminated water needs different handling from a clean supply line leak.

How Extraction and Drying Work

Crews remove standing water with pumps or extraction units, then use moisture meters to find wet materials behind and under what you can see. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are repeated until materials reach normal levels. Read about water mitigation for the drying stage and water damage repair for rebuilding afterward.

Garden home sections may have different layouts from larger lots. We do not state how they are built. Tell the dispatcher whether your home is attached, how many floors it has and where the water entered.

It also helps to have these details ready when you call: your street, the room where you first saw water, whether the water is still running, whether it looks clean or dirty, and whether anyone has turned off the main valve. The answers decide what equipment is sent and which safety steps come first. The published reference for drying work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company how its crews follow it.

Insurance and What Changes the Cost

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and generally exclude gradual leaks and floods. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help describe the loss. Your insurer decides coverage, so call early and keep receipts.

Price depends on the area affected, the water source, how long materials stayed wet, the flooring and wall types, and the equipment needed. Ask for the scope in writing. See water damage cleanup for what follows extraction, and mold remediation if growth is a concern.
