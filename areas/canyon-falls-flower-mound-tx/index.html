Water Damage Cleanup in Canyon Falls

Canyon Falls is a large community at the northern edge of Flower Mound. The Town of Flower Mound's homeowner association directory lists it, and the Town's subdivision index names many Canyon Falls villages, including 4A, 5, 6A, 6B, 8, 10A, 10B and 12. We do not claim official boundaries.

Town Limits, Mailing Addresses and Schools

Property listings for Canyon Falls homes show different mailing cities, and a neighborhood directory describes the area as blending into Argyle and Northlake. A mailing address does not settle which town you live in. The Town's own records list Canyon Falls, and your property tax and utility bills show who serves you.

That matters for service questions. Permits, public utilities and flood-map questions go to the town or utility that serves your address. If you are unsure, our Argyle page explains how that neighboring town handles water and permits.

Newer Homes Still Leak

A newer home does not prevent water damage. We do not know the age of any particular Canyon Falls home, and we do not claim that newer construction is more or less likely to leak. What stays true is that supply lines fail, appliance hoses split, water heaters leak and roofs let in storm water at any age.

If you recently moved in, find your main water shutoff now. Knowing where it is can decide how much damage a failed line causes. See appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup for common sources.

Safety Steps Before the Crew Arrives

First Safety Steps in Canyon Falls

These limit the damage while you wait. None of them should put you in danger.

Stop the waterClose the main shutoff, or the valve at the failed fixture, if you can reach it safely and the floor is dry.
Mind the electricityStay out of standing water near outlets, cords and appliances. Do not flip a breaker while standing in water.
Photograph firstTake photos and a short video of the damage before anything is moved. They support your insurance record.
Keep people clearKeep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, toilet or outside. Call 911 or the utility first for gas, sparks or smoke.

Creeks, Rain and Flood Questions

The Town's Flood Hazard Areas page names Graham Branch among watercourses with mapped hazard areas, and listing sites describe a preserve of that name near Canyon Falls. That does not put any home in a flood hazard area. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, and the Town offers a mapping tool and a floodplain manager for address checks.

The Texas Department of Insurance says home policies generally exclude floods, while most cover sudden and accidental water damage. For outside water, read storm water damage. For a roof source, see roof leak water damage.

Extraction, Drying and Cost

Crews remove standing water, map moisture with meters and run air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are normal. The price depends on the water source, the area affected, the time materials stayed wet and the equipment needed. Ask for the scope in writing before work starts. Drying decisions are described in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published reference, and any company should be able to say how it uses it.
