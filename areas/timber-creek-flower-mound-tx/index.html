Water Damage Cleanup in Timber Creek

Timber Creek is both a neighborhood name and the name of a watercourse, so it helps to separate them. The Town of Flower Mound's subdivision index lists many Timber Creek sections, including Timber Creek Addition No. 1 and No. 2, several Timber Creek community development sections, Timber Creek Garden Homes and Timber Creek Park through Phase VII. We do not claim official boundaries. A neighborhood directory places Timber Creek in the central part of town with Wellington.

What the Town Says About Creeks and Drainage

The Town's Flood Hazard Areas page lists Timber Creek among the named creeks whose areas are mapped as Special Flood Hazard Area, and it says the list is mainly, but not limited to, those locations. The same page says the town sits in the Denton and Elm Fork Trinity watersheds. A creek on that list is not a statement about any single home.

The Town's flood risk page adds a point worth knowing. It says flood risk is not limited to areas near a creek or Grapevine Lake, and that local drainage flooding can occur. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status for Timber Creek is not verified here. The Town offers a mapping tool and a floodplain manager for address checks.

A Documented Drainage Project Nearby

A Cross Timbers Gazette report from January 2019 said the Town started design work on drainage improvements for Waketon Road at Timber Creek. The road, between FM 2499 and Townsend Drive, floods during heavy rains. The plan described replacing four culverts with eight and improving about 1,500 feet of channel.

That is a road project, not a record of flooding in any home, and we do not know its current status. Treat it as proof that heavy-rain drainage is a known local issue, and that rain-driven water can reach homes by more than one route.

Storm Water Versus a Plumbing Leak

The cause decides the cleanup and the coverage. Water that entered from outside is handled as contaminated, and wet porous materials are often removed. A supply line or appliance failure is usually clean water that can be extracted and dried. See storm water damage and flood damage restoration for the outside-water cases, and emergency water removal for standing water.

The Texas Department of Insurance says home policies generally exclude floods, and flood protection requires a separate policy. It also says most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage such as a burst pipe. Call your insurer early, photograph everything and keep receipts. Your policy decides coverage.

What Changes the Cost

Cost depends on the water source, the area affected, the time materials stayed wet, the type of flooring and wall material, and the equipment needed. Water from outside adds removal and cleaning steps. Ask for the scope in writing. For the drying itself, the published reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, and a company should be able to explain how its crews use it.
