Water removal is the first physical step of any water loss. It is narrower than water mitigation, which also includes moisture mapping and structural drying. Removal gets the bulk of the water out, and drying finishes the job.

When Standing Water Is an Emergency

Water across a floor is already moving into baseboards and under walls. If it is rising, entering from outside, or reaches electrical outlets, treat it as an emergency. Water that came from a toilet, drain or outside flooding may carry bacteria and should not be walked through.

What to do before the crew arrives

Never step into standing water if outlets, power strips or appliances are submerged. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground.

If you smell gas or see sparking, leave the home and call 911 or the utility before calling the restoration line.

Keep children and pets out of the water, especially if it came from a drain, toilet or outside.

Burst Pipe Water Damage

A burst pipe under a sink, behind a wall or in the attic is one of the most common reasons for an emergency call. Water can run for an hour or more before anyone notices, and attic lines drop water into ceilings that then sag and fail. The first move is the main shutoff, usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house.

Once the water is off, crews extract what is on the floor, open any bulging ceiling sections to release trapped water and set drying equipment. The line that failed still needs a plumber. Restoration handles the water, and a plumber handles the pipe, so expect to coordinate both.

Freeze events raise the risk. The National Weather Service recorded a low of minus 2 degrees Fahrenheit at DFW on February 16, 2021, after a long stretch at or below freezing. Homeowners who know where their main shutoff is can limit a pipe failure in minutes.

Extraction removes the water you can see, but it does not dry the structure. A thin film left in carpet pad, subfloor and wall cavities is what causes odor and mold weeks later. That is why removal flows directly into drying, and the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard treats drying to normal readings as part of the job. TDI also notes that mold can start growing within a day after a leak.

Crawl Space and Lower-Level Water

Water in a crawl space or lower level is often not visible from the living area. Callers describe water coming up from irrigation backflow, a failed line or heavy rain. Crews pump the space, check insulation and subfloor underside, and set equipment to dry the framing above.

If the water came in from outside during a storm, the cause may fall outside homeowner coverage. Check your policy, and see flood damage restoration for how outside water is treated differently from a plumbing failure.

What Affects the Cost of Water Removal

  • Access: stairs, crawl spaces and tight rooms take more time.
