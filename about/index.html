About Flower Mound Water Damage Pros

A local 24/7 dispatch line for water damage and mold, built around plain explanations and scope and price before work starts.

☎ (817) 623-9267

What This Business Is

Flower Mound Water Damage Pros is a dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in Flower Mound, Texas and the nearby towns of Lewisville, Argyle and Grapevine. When you call, a dispatcher asks what you see, gives safety steps to follow while you wait and connects you with independent restoration professionals.

We do not publish names, awards or review counts, because none of them would help you decide in the middle of a water loss. What we can publish is how the process works and what you should expect, and the rest of this page does that.

How a Call Works

  1. You call the 24/7 line and describe what is happening.
  2. The dispatcher gives safety steps, such as shutting off the main valve and staying out of water near outlets.
  3. A crew is routed to your address, and same-day response is available when crews are free.
  4. The crew explains the scope and the price before work starts.
  5. Photos and moisture readings are logged, so you have documentation for your insurer.

What We Will and Will Not Promise

We will tell you the truth about the stage you need. If water is still moving, we say so and send you to removal first. If mold is involved, we explain that Texas requires TDLR-licensed assessors and remediators and that the same company generally cannot do both on one project.

We will not promise an arrival time, because traffic, weather and crew availability change it. We will not promise that an insurer will approve a claim, because the policy and the cause decide that. We will not promise that water never returns after restoration, because restoration repairs damage and does not fix drainage or foundation problems.

Standards We Point To

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 is the standard for mold remediation. We name them because homeowners can ask any company whether its crews work to them. Naming a standard is not a claim that any company holds a certification, so ask each crew directly.

The Texas Department of Insurance and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation are the state sources we rely on for insurance and mold rules. Our guides link every insurance and licensing statement back to what those agencies say.

What the Dispatcher Asks and Why

The first questions are about safety, not paperwork. Is the water still running, is any of it near outlets or a panel, and does anything smell like gas or sewage? Those answers decide whether you are told to leave the home, to call the utility first or to shut off the main valve and wait for a crew.

The next questions are about the damage itself. Which rooms are wet, what the floors and walls are made of and how long the water has been there tell the crew which equipment to bring. A wet hardwood floor needs different drying equipment than soaked carpet, and a ceiling that has started to sag is handled differently from a damp wall.

Why Scope and Price Come First

Water damage jobs often go wrong when the price shows up after the work. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, in plain words, so you can compare it with what your policy covers. The factors that move the price are the category of the water, the area affected, the materials involved, the days of drying and the amount of rebuild.

That is also why each stage is scoped separately. Removal, drying, cleanup and repair are different jobs with different prices, and a homeowner who sees them separately can ask better questions. The services page lays out each stage and when it applies.
