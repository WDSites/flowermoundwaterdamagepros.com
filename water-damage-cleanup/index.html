Cleanup is the middle stage. It follows emergency water removal and comes before the rebuild covered in water damage repair. The goal is a clean, dry and sanitary space that is ready for repair.

Why the First 48 Hours Matter

Wet porous materials turn from a drying job into a disposal job quickly. Carpet pad, insulation and soft drywall that stay wet for a couple of days often cannot be saved, and the Texas Department of Insurance warns that mold can start growing within a day after a leak. A fast, thorough cleanup reduces what has to be thrown out.

Clean water from a supply line behaves differently from water that has touched a floor drain, a toilet or the outdoors. The category of water decides what cleanup involves, and it is the first thing a technician checks.

The Cleanup Sequence

  1. Remove wet materials that cannot be dried: pad, insulation, soaked drywall and swollen trim.
  2. Bag and haul out debris so it does not sit in the home.
  3. Clean surfaces that stayed in place with detergent and the right antimicrobial product.
  4. Treat wall cavities and subfloor edges exposed by removal.
  5. Control odor at the source rather than masking it.
  6. Verify that surfaces are clean and readings are heading to dry before closing anything up.

Cleanup and drying overlap. Equipment from water mitigation usually runs while cleanup is under way, so the structure is drying as it is cleaned. Closing a wall over a surface that has not been cleaned and dried is one of the most common reasons for smells that return.

Odor After a Leak Means Something Is Still Wet

A smell near a cabinet, closet or stairwell after a leak usually points to damp material you cannot see. Callers describe a bad smell near basement stairs, in a closet beside the air handler or under a sink. Air fresheners and ozone machines cover it temporarily, but the smell comes back until the wet source is found and removed.

A thermal camera and moisture meter find the source in minutes. If the material is salvageable, it is dried and treated, and if not, it is removed. If you find visible mold during cleanup, the work changes, and the section on mold remediation explains how that process is handled in Texas.

What Water Damage Cleanup Does Not Include

Cleanup is not waterproofing. A caller with water coming in through a foundation or wall needs a foundation or drainage specialist for the cause, and restoration only handles the damage. Cleanup also does not rebuild finished surfaces. Once the space is clean and dry, repair takes over.

Ask the crew whether they handle contents such as furniture and electronics, and what they leave to you. Photograph everything before it is moved and keep damaged items until your insurance adjuster has seen them, as TDI advises.

What Affects the Cost of Water Damage Cleanup

  • Water category: clean water is simpler than gray water or sewage.
  • Area affected and how many rooms the water reached.
  • Materials removed: carpet and pad cost less to remove than tile, cabinets or plaster.
  • Time between the loss and the start of cleanup.
  • Amount of debris and the access to haul it out.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. For your real number, call the 24/7 line. For insurance, TDI says sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks are generally excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer.
