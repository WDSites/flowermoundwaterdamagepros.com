The two jobs are separate and both are needed. A plumber stops the leak at its source, but a new heater does nothing for the wet drywall, baseboard and flooring left behind. That damp material is what we dry.

How a Water Heater Leak Starts and Where the Water Goes

A steel storage tank slowly corrodes from the inside, and when the wall finally gives way the whole tank can release at once. That is a rupture. Other leaks are smaller: a loose fitting on the hot or cold line, a drain valve that weeps, or a temperature and pressure relief valve that discharges because pressure built up inside the tank.

The path of the water depends on where the heater sits. If yours is in a garage, water usually runs across the floor toward the lowest point and wicks into the bottom of the wall. In a hallway closet, it soaks the carpet or flooring outside the door. In an attic, it drops through insulation and drywall into the ceiling below.

A drain pan under the heater catches small drips only. A pan with a drain line carries overflow outside, but a pan with a clogged or missing line spills over its rim the moment a tank splits.

Water Heater Leak Safety: The First Hour

  1. Close the cold water inlet valve above the heater. Turn the handle until it stops. This also stops new water refilling the tank.
  2. Switch off power to an electric heater at the breaker, but only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  3. For a gas heater, turn the gas control to off only if you can do it safely. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility first.
  4. Do not touch the relief valve discharge pipe or the drain valve. Water from a tank can scald.
  5. Stay out of water that reaches outlets, power strips or the panel. Call the utility or 911 before calling us.
  6. Photograph the heater, the label and the damage before anything moves.

Hot water counts for something here. Water that has just left a tank is clean supply water, but if it sat in a pan or a wall for days it can pick up debris and odor. We note how long the water has been present, because it decides whether materials dry in place or come out.

How Our Crew Cleans Up and Dries the Area

1

Portable extractors and a wet vacuum pull water from carpet, tile and the garage floor, including the pan area.

2

Open the wall base

We pull baseboard and may drill small holes or cut a short strip of drywall at the bottom to reach wet insulation.

3

Map moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show which studs, plates and flooring are still wet.

4

Dry

Air movers push air across the base of the wall while a low grain refrigerant dehumidifier removes moisture from the room air.

5

Verify

We take readings each visit and compare them with an unaffected spot until the area is back to normal.

Ask any restorer about ANSI/IICRC S500 and whether its crews follow it on a leaking tank job. Daily readings are the proof that drying is finished, and your adjuster may ask for them. For the full extraction side, see emergency water removal.

Insurance and a Water Heater Leak

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage. A tank that ruptures overnight fits that description. A slow seep that went on for months is a gradual leak, and gradual leaks and seepage are generally excluded.

That split is why timing and evidence matter. Photograph or film the damage before repairs, and keep the failed heater or part until the adjuster has seen it. TDI also says to report hidden damage within days of first noticing it, so call your insurer early and ask whether the heater itself is part of the claim.

Our crew records photos and moisture readings for your claim. We do not decide coverage. Your insurer does, and your policy language controls the answer.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Heater Leak

  • Draining the tank into the house to relieve pressure.
  • Restoring power to the heater before the tank is full again.
  • Rolling up wet carpet but leaving the pad and baseboard in place.
  • Setting a household fan and calling it done while the wall base stays wet.
  • Discarding the failed heater before the adjuster looks at it.
