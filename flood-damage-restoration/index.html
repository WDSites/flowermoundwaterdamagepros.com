Outside water, groundwater and sewage backups are handled differently from a broken pipe. Dispatch is open around the clock.

Flood damage restoration in Flower Mound, TX is the work of removing storm, creek or groundwater and sewage backup from a home, then drying, cleaning and repairing what it touched. Flood water is not clean, and it is handled as contaminated water. Dispatch is open 24/7. While you wait, stay out of the water, shut off power only if the panel is dry and reachable, and call 911 or the utility first if you smell gas or see sparking.

Flood damage differs from a plumbing leak because the water comes from outside the supply system. It carries soil, bacteria and debris, and it often reaches the home slowly and stays for days. That changes what can be saved, what the crew wears and what your insurance covers.

When Flood Water Is an Emergency

Water entering the home from outside, rising in a crawl space or backing up from a floor drain is always an emergency. Do not walk through it. Callers describe water coming into a crawl space from an irrigation line or heavy rain, and some describe an entire floor under water. The response starts with safety, then removal, then drying.

What to do while you wait

  • Stay out of the water, especially near outlets and appliances.
  • Turn off power only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Otherwise leave and call the utility.
  • Move valuables, documents and electronics to higher levels.
  • Keep children and pets away from the water and from anything it touched.
  • Take photos and video before moving or discarding anything.

Flower Mound Flood Exposure: Check Before You Assume

Flower Mound's southern edge runs along Grapevine Lake and Marshall Creek, and the Denton Creek District sits on the western edge. Living near water does not mean a home floods, and living away from it does not mean a home is safe. The right move is to check the FEMA flood map for your address.

The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you enter an address and see the mapped flood zone. If your home is in or near a mapped zone, ask your insurer about a separate flood policy. The Texas Department of Insurance says home policies do not cover floods, including mold that comes from a flood.

Flood Restoration, Step by Step

1

Secure

Confirm electrical and gas safety before anyone enters.

2

Remove

Pump standing water and take out contaminated porous materials.

3

Disinfect

Clean and treat surfaces that stay in place.

4

Dry

Run air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are normal.

Because flood water is contaminated, crews are more aggressive about removing porous materials like carpet, pad and lower drywall. Crews commonly cut drywall above the highest wet reading rather than at it. That can look like overkill, but it is what prevents odor and mold later, and ANSI/IICRC S500 treats flood water as Category 3.

When the Water Is Sewage

Water from a toilet, tub or floor drain is Category 3, which means contaminated. Stay out of the area, keep children and pets away and do not run water in the house, because it can add to the backup. Protective gear and removal of porous materials are part of the job, and the full steps are on the page for sewage backup cleanup.

Sewage Backup: What to Do First

Raw sewage from a floor drain or toilet is Category 3 water. Stay out of the area, keep children and pets away and do not run water in the house, because it can add to the backup. Call the restoration line and, if the backup comes from the main line, your utility or a plumber for the blockage.

Crews wear protective gear, extract the sewage, remove porous materials that touched it and disinfect what remains. Carpet, pad and lower drywall are usually discarded. Ask the crew how they will contain the area so the contamination does not spread to clean rooms.

What Affects the Cost of Flood Restoration

  • Water category: flood and sewage water require more removal and disinfection.
  • Depth and square footage affected.
  • Materials involved: carpet, hardwood, tile, insulation and cabinets.
  • Drying days and the equipment needed.
  • Rebuild scope once the structure is dry.
